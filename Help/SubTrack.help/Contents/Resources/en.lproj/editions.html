Choose between the two versions of SubTrack

SubTrack comes in two builds: one from the Mac App Store, one downloaded from the web. They slim files identically — same rules, same presets, same stream copying, same output, byte for byte. For the thing the app exists to do, there is no difference between them.

Three capabilities separate them, and all three are App Store policy rather than anything SubTrack chose. If you arrived here by clicking a “?” next to a dimmed control, that control is one of the three.

Find out which version you have

  1. Choose SubTrack ▸ Settings (⌘,) and click Encoding.
  2. Read the Codec set line. Full (all codecs) is the downloadable version; App Store (VideoToolbox) is the App Store version.

The App Store version also has no Updates tab in Settings, because the store handles its updates.

What the App Store version can’t do

Software H.264 and H.265 encoding. The App Store build bundles an FFmpeg licensed under the LGPL, without libx264 and libx265 — those are GPL, and the store’s terms and the GPL pull against each other. Video encoding is done by VideoToolbox, the hardware encoder in your Mac, which handles both H.264 and HEVC and is much faster than software encoding. The software encoders fit more quality into the same file size; the hardware ones finish sooner. You’ll meet this in Settings ▸ FFmpeg ▸ Supported Formats, where the codec list carries a Missing codecs? link, and in the warning you get if you choose a transcode target this build can’t encode.

Pointing SubTrack at your own FFmpeg. In Settings ▸ FFmpeg, the App Store version shows Built-in and the line “Using a custom ffmpeg is available in the downloadable version.” The downloadable version puts a Built-in / Custom… choice there instead, with an Auto-detect button that finds the FFmpeg builds already installed on your Mac. A sandboxed app can’t run an executable you supply, and the App Store version is sandboxed; the downloadable one isn’t, which is what lets it do this.

Installing the subtrack command-line tool. In Settings ▸ CLI, the App Store version says “Installing the command-line tool is available in the downloadable version” where the downloadable one offers Install…. Copying a binary into a folder on your PATH is outside the sandbox.

Beyond those three, the downloadable version checks GitHub for its own new releases and can tell you one is out. The App Store version doesn’t need to.

Neither version is the crippled one. Slimming — dropping tracks and copying the rest — is untouched by all of this, and transcoding still works in both; it’s the encoder behind it that differs.

Get the other version

The downloadable version is notarized by Apple and installed from a disk image; you update it yourself when it tells you there’s a new release. The App Store version installs and updates like everything else on your Mac.

  • The SubTrack website, which links to both.
  • SubTrack releases on GitHub, for the downloadable version and its notes.

You can keep both installed. They share the same iCloud container, so your presets sync between them just as they sync between Macs.
