What SubTrack sends, and what it doesn’t

Almost nothing leaves your Mac. There are no accounts, no advertising identifiers, and no tracking. What follows is the complete list of what does go out, and where each of those things goes.

Your video files never leave your Mac

Everything SubTrack does to a file — reading its tracks, working out what to keep, writing the slimmed copy — happens on your own machine. The FFmpeg bundled inside SubTrack is compiled with networking disabled, so it isn’t a matter of the app choosing not to upload anything: that FFmpeg contains no network code and cannot open a connection at all. Your files, their names, and their contents are never sent anywhere.

Crash and performance reports go to Sentry

When SubTrack crashes or runs badly, it sends a report to Sentry, an error-monitoring service, so the fault can be found and fixed. A report carries device information, the state the app was in when things went wrong, and performance traces.

It is not linked to your identity. SubTrack tells Sentry not to attach your IP address, your device name, or anything else of that kind, and there is no account to attach a report to in the first place. Reports from debug builds and from automated test runs are discarded before they’re sent.

Presets sync through your own iCloud, and nothing else does

Saved presets sync between your Macs through CloudKit, in the private iCloud container belonging to your Apple Account. That container is yours: the developer cannot read it, and what’s stored there is governed by Apple’s privacy policy.

Presets are the only thing that syncs. Your queues, the files you put in them, and your output folders are stored on this Mac and go nowhere.

Turn off the update check

The version downloaded directly from GitHub asks GitHub’s public list of releases whether a newer one exists. That request reveals what any web request reveals to a server — your IP address, mainly — and nothing else about you. To stop it:

  1. Choose SubTrack ▸ Settings (⌘,) and click Updates.
  2. Set Check for updates to Never.

The Mac App Store version never makes this request — the store keeps it current — so it has no Updates settings at all.

The document that governs

This page is a summary written for people using the app. The SubTrack privacy policy is the document that actually governs, and it’s the one to read if you need the exact wording.
