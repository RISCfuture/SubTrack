Set how many files encode at once

Choose SubTrack ▸ Settings (⌘,) and click Encoding.

Simultaneous encodes

This caps how many files SubTrack works on at the same time. The choices step from one to eight rather than offering every number, because the meaningful decision is “one at a time”, “a couple”, or “use the whole machine” — the values in between don’t behave differently enough to matter.

For ordinary slimming, this setting barely matters. Copying tracks is limited by how fast your disk can read and write, not by your processor. Running eight copies at once mostly makes them queue up for the same disk, and can be slower than running two.

It matters when tracks are being converted. Converting is real processor work, so several at once genuinely use more of the machine. If your queues routinely convert — because your sources use codecs outside your preferred list — raising the limit will finish a batch sooner.

The trade is responsiveness. A high limit while converting will make the rest of the Mac feel sluggish. If you’re working while a batch runs, keep it low.

Lowering the limit doesn’t interrupt anything already running. Files in flight finish; the new limit applies to what starts next.

Codec set

This reports which FFmpeg build your copy of SubTrack ships with. It isn’t a setting — there’s nothing to change — it’s there so you know what you have.

  • Full (all codecs) — the downloadable version. Includes the software H.264 and H.265 encoders.
  • App Store (VideoToolbox) — the App Store version. Hardware encoding through VideoToolbox, without the software encoders, whose licence App Store distribution doesn’t permit.

Both slim files identically. The difference only appears when a track has to be converted, and only for certain target codecs. See Choose between the two versions of SubTrack.

For the full list of what the build you’re running can read and write, open Settings ▸ FFmpeg and click Supported Formats.
